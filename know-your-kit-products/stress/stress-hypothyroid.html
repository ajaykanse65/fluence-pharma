Stress, Hypothyroid

  • Hypothyroidism is a state of increased oxidative stress. Current research shows that there is a rising incidence of hair loss associated with a thyroid imbalance. More and more subclinical, covert deficiencies of vitamins and minerals have been identified in hypothyroidism.
  • Starvation, quantity and quality of protein, caloric restriction, vitamin and mineral deficiencies, as well as over nutrition alters the endocrine gland function. Antioxidants, iron, zinc, selenium, vitamins A and D are all important requirements for thyroid function as well as hair growth.
  • Stress causes nutritional imbalance and metabolic imbalance. Hair growth during such adaptation is arrested in order to conserve the nutrients and redirect them to more important vital functions.
  • Stress leads to neurochemical, neurotransmitter and hormonal alterations through sympathetic nervous system, hypothalamic – pituatory – adrenal (HPA) axis and substance P, mediated by stress.
  • Elevation in allostatic load (chronic stress lead to the wear and tear of cells) causes sleep, deprivation, increase appetite, increase blood pressure, risk of cardiovascular disease and metabolic syndrome.
  • Stress escalates physiological demand for energy, oxygen utilization, cellular functions, requiring enzymes, cofactors, catalysts and micronutrients.
  • Stress induced hair loss is mediated by substance P which is known to act through ROS. Antioxidants can counteract the effects of ROS and mitigate hair loss.
